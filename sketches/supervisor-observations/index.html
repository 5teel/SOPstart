PHASE 34 SKETCH FOR REVIEW — NOT BUILT

Supervisor Observations

What an observation is, why it exists, where it's recorded, and what the worker sees.

00

What is an observation? Why does it exist?

Definition: a timestamped, unchangeable record made by a supervisor that says "I stood at the machine and watched [worker] perform [SOP]. Verdict: performed to SOP / needs support. Optional note." It takes ~30 seconds to record. It can never be edited or deleted (same immutability as your sign-off chain).

Why it should exist — four reasons:
1. It answers Visy's #1 named pain: fraudulent / shared sign-offs. Today every record in SOPstart is worker-initiated — a worker taps through a walkthrough and says "I did it". Two workers can share a phone; a signature proves someone tapped, not that someone watched. An observation is the only record that is supervisor-initiated: it's the supervisor's own account, on their own login, that they personally witnessed the work. That's the counter-evidence that makes the whole record set trustworthy.
2. It's the legal evidence layer (HSWA due diligence). When WorkSafe NZ or an auditor asks "how do you know your people are competent?", "they read the SOP once" is a weak answer. "A supervisor observed them doing it, here's the dated record" is the strong answer — it demonstrates people were correctly shown, by competent people.
3. It's the missing middle of competency. Right now there's nothing between "read the SOP" and "signed off". Phase 35's training matrix needs a "watched doing it correctly" state — observations are the raw data that state derives from. Without Phase 34, the matrix can only say read / not-read.
4. Complacency reset for long-tenured workers. Dave has run the beader for 12 years and hasn't looked at the SOP since induction. A periodic 30-second observation ("still consistent with SOP" or "needs reset — shortcuts creeping in") is the industry-standard mechanism for catching drift before it becomes an incident.

02

Entry point B — /activity supervisor view

Alternative (or addition): a Record observation button in the header of the existing supervisor activity feed, plus a per-row shortcut on completions — "I watched this walkthrough happen, record it as observed". Cheapest to build (no new surface), but the supervisor must pick both worker and SOP, and /activity is completion-centric — ad-hoc floor observations ("I watched Dave, no walkthrough running") fit awkwardly here.

03

The 30-second recording flow (desktop modal)

Same modal regardless of entry point. Three fields, two of which are often pre-filled: worker (pre-filled from person panel / activity row), SOP (searchable picker, scoped to SOPs the worker is required to know), verdict (two big buttons — your binary choice), optional note. The current SOP version is stamped automatically. Append-only: once saved there is no edit and no delete, which is stated right on the form so supervisors know it's evidence, not a chat message.

04

What the worker sees — their own profile

Trust is the whole game. If observations are secret supervisor notes, workers will (rightly) treat them as surveillance, and under the NZ Privacy Act workers are entitled to see records held about them anyway. So worker visibility ships in the same change as the supervisor write UI: a new section on the worker's own /profile page listing every observation about them, verdict and note included. Side effect: knowing the worker will read it keeps supervisor notes professional and coaching-toned.
