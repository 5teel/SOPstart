# SOPstart design review: emil-design-eng lens

Material reviewed: 18 live screenshots from `.planning/evals/latest/` (desktop: 63-home-worker-desktop, 63-home-map-site, 63-home-read-admin, 63-home-section-record, 58-walk-hazard, 58-walk-ppe, 58-walk-photo-required, 58-edit-admin, 58-edit-publish-dialog, 59-inbox, 59-signoff-open, 59-reject-dialog, 59-people; phone: 63-home-phone-list, 63-home-phone-map, 63-home-phone-read, 63-home-phone-signoffs, 58-phone-walk). Code read: `src/components/home/`, `src/components/focus/`, `src/components/office/`, `src/styles/blueprint-theme.css`, `src/app/globals.css`, ADR-0004 and ADR-0006. The skill is a single SKILL.md with no reference files.

## 1. Lens

Emil Kowalski's design engineering puts the "unseen details" first: every press, open, close and state change should feel heard and physically plausible, using custom ease-out curves, 100-300 ms timings, `transform`/`opacity` only, and press feedback on everything you can tap. Taste here means cohesion and good defaults. One shared, well-tuned primitive beats sixteen hand-rolled ones, and motion is decided by how often the user sees it and what it is for.

## 2. Verdict

The static layout is calm and mostly avoids template slop. The "AI-generic" feel comes from the interaction layer: nothing responds to a press, dialogs and steps swap without any transition, and each surface rolls its own overlay, chip and callout instead of sharing one tuned default. The result looks like a well-specified wireframe that nobody has *touched*. The few remaining visual tells (sparkle-purple AI card, tinted left-stripe callouts, icon-in-a-circle empty state) are the stock component shapes an LLM reaches for first.

## 5. What already works

- **The Start fusion is the right kind of delight, rationed correctly.** The full sequence plays on the first Start of the day, a short one (×0.3) after, and nothing under reduced motion. It runs on WAAPI (off the main thread, `ReadView.tsx:95`), never blocks input, and uses named `--dur-fuse-*` tokens. This is the skill's frequency and purpose framework applied almost verbatim.
- **The one-thing-per-screen walk.** One step alone, a large type size (`text-step`), and a full-width 60 px primary in a sticky thumb-zone dock (`WalkStep.tsx:145-149`) with safe-area padding. The skeleton is excellent. It only needs the press and transition details from recommendations 1 and 3.
- **Reduced motion is respected everywhere motion exists.** `motion-reduce:animate-none` on every skeleton, a `prefers-reduced-motion` block for the route and step classes and for the map plates, and pending-nav spinners delayed 120 ms so prefetched navigations never flash (`globals.css:38-58`).

## 6. Skill fit

The skill fits well for the interaction layer: press feedback, unanimated state changes and borrowed defaults are exactly what makes this app feel machine-made, and its frequency-first motion rules suit a tool used dozens of times a shift. It fits poorly as a visual or safety authority: it says nothing about contrast in sunlight, glove-sized targets or hazard signalling, and its marketing-flavoured ideas (springs, stagger, decorative delight) should stay out of the walk, where speed and certainty beat charm.
